# Persona 14 — MIKE (Competitor Comparator)

**Who:** Mike, 33, project manager. Two years running Instagram/DM automation on a leading competitor (ManyChat, Chatfuel, Instagram's native automation, etc.). Evaluating InstaSetter as a possible switch for his team's IG inbox.

**Lens:** Feature checklist. Does it have what my current tool has? Is the convention familiar, or do I have to re-learn muscle memory? Is there an import path for my existing flows? Would switching cost me a sprint of productivity? What, if anything, is actually _better_ here — and is it worth the switching cost?

**Frustration triggers:**

- Missing table-stakes features (no broadcast, no audience segmentation, no analytics dashboard, no template library, no schedule, no integrations tab).
- Different conventions for no apparent reason ("Flow" vs "Bot" vs "Block" — why three words for the same concept?).
- No "Import from ManyChat" path. No migration tooling at all.
- Broken core surfaces on a first visit (the Conversations inbox throwing "Something went wrong" is a bridge-out on the tour).
- No onboarding — I landed on a two-button splash page with no pitch, no feature tour, no proof the product works.

## Category Summary

### Navigation & Flow

The IA collapses to two buttons on home, one button on dashboard, and a single hardcoded flow URL. No bot list, no broadcasts, no audience, no integrations, no settings. Compared to ManyChat's ~8 top-level concepts (Automation, Live Chat, Audience, Broadcasting, Growth Tools, Ads, Settings, Account) this is barely a dashboard. **Score: Blocker-level gap.**

### Copy & Labels

Three overlapping terms ("Flow", "Bot", "Block") used interchangeably. Dashboard self-describes as "preview" and "prototype" — buyer-hostile. Error states are generic JavaScript defaults. One word-labels on tab glyphs. **Score: High-Critical across the board.**

### Forms & Input

Block inspector has no required/optional indicators, no dirty-state pill, no autosave, no validation. Publishing has no confirmation gate. For a tool that edits _live_ DM automation that talks to real prospects, this is unacceptably permissive. **Score: Critical.**

### Visual & Layout

Flow builder has good bones — three-panel layout, block palette, inspector, canvas — but inspector and simulator both want the right rail and collide on 1280×800. Tabs row has a z-index bug that blocks normal clicks. **Score: Critical (tab click bug), High (panel collision).**

### Feedback & State

No analytics dashboard (the #1 reason my boss approves tool purchases), no toast system, no autosave indicator, no loading skeletons, no success confirmations. The conversations inbox error state doesn't actually retry on "Try again". **Score: Blocker (no analytics).**

### Trust & Safety

No pricing, no terms, no privacy, no footer, no company info on the landing page. No logout, no account menu, no workspace switcher, no connected-account list anywhere in the app. Publish has no confirmation. Console errors bleed to the client. **Score: Critical to Blocker.**

### Accessibility

Glyph-only icons on primary tab row. Multiple unlabelled "×" buttons. Unknown keyboard shortcut coverage. Likely screen-reader hostile given the exploration log shows pointer-event interception on the tab row (z-index bug almost always correlates with poor focus management). **Score: High.**

## Switching Verdict

**Would I switch?** No — not today, and not in the next quarter.

**Why:**

1. The conversations inbox — the single feature this product is named after — is broken on first visit.
2. No broadcasts, sequences, or audience segmentation means this is 30% of a competitor's feature set. Even if the flow builder were perfect, my re-engagement workflows live in ManyChat's Broadcasting tab, and there's no analogue here.
3. No analytics = no ROI story to my CFO.
4. No import path = I'd rebuild 40 flows by hand.
5. Landing + dashboard are splash screens, not a product — I can't demo this to my team.

**What would _make_ me switch:**

1. Lead the marketing with the AI-first prompt-inspector + simulator story. That's the one genuinely novel thing here and it's buried four clicks deep. ManyChat literally cannot match it.
2. Publish a "Import from ManyChat" button that accepts their JSON export. Even a 60%-accurate import is enough to de-risk the trial.
3. Build the missing table-stakes surfaces (inbox, broadcasts, analytics) to rough parity with ManyChat. Not 10× better — just "not embarrassing".
4. Lean into versioning as a differentiator. "Git for your bots" is a pitch every technical marketer understands and nobody else offers.

**The positive that actually stuck with me:** the prompt inspector is a real thing. A marketer who can read and tune the system prompt that goes to Claude — with the rationale ("Why this exists / Zero qualification attempted in many conversations that died at value delivery") inline — is a 10× leap over what ManyChat's "AI" can do. If the rest of the product caught up with this one feature, I'd switch tomorrow. Right now, this feature is a great demo stranded inside a half-finished app.
